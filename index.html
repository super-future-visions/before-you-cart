How we calculate this, and how sure we are

Impact = garment weight × the material’s impact per kilogram, plus an allowance for making and shipping. Buying secondhand counts as 10% of that, since the item already exists. We show ranges because real products vary.

Status of these numbers: sourcing is under way. The cotton and polyester rows now cite published studies, but those studies measure different stages (fiber, fabric, or a whole T-shirt), so figures aren’t directly comparable. Where a bound isn’t sourced yet, the Source column says so, and rows marked Pending are still working estimates. To compare materials on one basis, we treat fabric as fiber plus about 3–7 kg CO₂e per kg for spinning, weaving or knitting, dyeing and finishing, a range inferred from the cotton and polyester studies above. For materials with little published data (silk, cashmere, hemp, linen, acrylic, recycled cotton) we scaled values using one database’s ratios to its own cotton figure, because its absolute numbers run several times higher than the studies cited here, probably because of a different system boundary. Those rows have wide ranges and need the most thorough review, as does anything marked Low. Garment weights and making-and-shipping allowances are also our own estimates. Please don’t cite any of these figures yet.

Why ranges and confidence labels, not one score

Scoring materials is contested. In June 2022 the Norwegian Consumer Authority concluded that the consumer-facing use of the Higg Materials Sustainability Index was potentially misleading, and its owner paused that use globally. An independent review by KPMG later found the index could be prone to misinterpretation when used on its own, and the single score has since been retired. We take two lessons: show uncertainty openly, and never reduce a garment to one unexplained number.

What’s not included yet

Washing, drying and ironing, disposal, packaging, returns, dyeing differences between factories, and brand-specific supply chains. Garment weights are typical values, not measurements of your item.

Where we’ll verify numbers

Peer-reviewed lifecycle assessments, Textile Exchange materials reporting, brand and industry lifecycle disclosures, and Cascale’s Higg data with its known limits in mind. These are the planned source types, not yet applied to the figures above.

Measuring this page…

About these numbers. They are rounded, category-level estimates drawn from published lifecycle assessments and industry reports, not measurements of a specific brand. Real products vary a lot with fabric, factory and shipping, which is why you see ranges. Treat this prototype as a conversation starter, and check the sources before relying on any figure.

Everyday comparisons use about 60 liters of water per shower and about 0.25 kg CO₂e per kilometer driven in an average car.
